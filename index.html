The Exit Gap · Research note · 12 October 2026

When a liquidation has no buyer, lenders pay

On-chain lending protects lenders with one assumption: if a loan goes bad, someone will buy the collateral. When nobody can, the loan stays open and the loss moves to the people who deposited.

What happens

1The collateral falls

A loan drops below its safety margin and becomes eligible for liquidation.

2Liquidators check the exit

They only act if selling the collateral repays the loan. Where the market for it is thin, the sale loses money, so nobody acts.

3The loan stays open

The bad loan sits in the market. Then one of two things happens.

A private buyer steps inA firm with its own balance sheet takes the collateral and holds it. Lenders are repaid. The risk now sits with one wallet.
Nobody steps inThe market fills to 100% borrowed, lenders can't withdraw, and the shortfall is eventually written off against them.

Both have already happened

PT-reUSD on Morpho, Ethereum · 25 August 2026

One buyer saved the lenders

A 2.8% price move set off a wave of liquidations. Before the move, on-chain markets could absorb about $5M of the collateral. Liquidators repaid $36.1M of loans, and one of them took about 96% of the collateral onto its own books instead of selling it.

A yield-vault token on Morpho, HyperEVM · June 2026 to today

Nobody did, and lenders are still waiting

The collateral was priced at its issuer's own reported value, so when the token crashed, no liquidation ever triggered. The loans stayed open. Lenders have had nothing to withdraw since 22 June, and the borrow rate has sat at its maximum ever since.

$9.5Mborrowed at the peak
$0.71Mcollateral value today
$0withdrawable since 22 June

Outright losses are rare. In six lending incidents we studied from 2025 and 2026, none of the lender losses came from a missing buyer alone. They came from oracles that never moved and from exploits. Where the buyer was missing, as with PT-reUSD, one firm chose to absorb it. Lenders were protected by that choice, not by the market's design.

Who pays

Lenders and vault depositorsFirst and directly. Their money is stuck while the loan sits open, then written down.
CuratorsThe vaults that allocated into the market take the outflows and the reputational hit.
Issuers and chainsRisk teams respond by cutting caps. When Plasma's $300M backstop ended, Aave took its WETH cap from 120,000 to 1. Smaller caps mean less borrowing against the asset.

The everyday cost is the third one. Most of the time nothing breaks. Caps stay low because the exit is thin, and the asset grows slower than it could.

What closes the gap

Turn the buyer that saved PT-reUSD lenders from luck into a commitment. Buy capacity sized to the largest liquidation a market can produce, posted as resting orders near fair value, funded by the issuer or the chain that wants the market to grow, and measured in public so lenders and risk teams can count it.

Method. Every Morpho market with at least $5M borrowed, pulled 12 Oct 2026 15:21 UTC from Morpho's API. 46 markets were left out because their reported collateral is below their debt: mispriced tokens, or loans already stuck. The 5% queue is the collateral seizable from every open position whose health factor falls below 1 after a 5% oracle drop, capped at debt times the liquidation incentive. "Can be sold" is the largest sale on the same chain whose live route proceeds still repay the debt (proceeds of at least the sale divided by the liquidation incentive, LIF = min(1.15, 1 / (1 − 0.3 × (1 − LLTV)))). Routes from KyberSwap and Pendle, cross-checked against Velora. Gas, redemption queues and off-chain buyers are not counted. Markets on Katana, Stable have no aggregator coverage and are not graded. Case studies rebuilt from on-chain state at the event blocks; Second case re-read 12 October 2026.
