How we rate exits

An Exit Rating answers one question for one lending market: if the collateral price drops 5%, how much collateral becomes liquidatable, and how much of it can be sold on the same chain today at a price that still repays the loan?

NQ means less than $25,000 of collateral would be liquidatable at a 5% drop, so there is nothing material to test. Today: 22 A, 2 B, 2 C, 8 D, 15 F and 38 NQ. The grade rates the exit for one market on one chain. It is not a view of the asset, the issuer's credit or the borrower.

How it works

  1. Read every position

    For each Morpho market with at least $5M borrowed, read every open borrower position and its health factor from Morpho's API.

  2. Stress the loan, not the market

    Apply a 5% drop to the price the market's oracle reports. Any position whose health factor falls below 1 becomes liquidatable. The exit is not shocked. It is measured as it is today.

  3. Build the queue

    Morpho lets a liquidator take a whole position. The collateral a liquidator can seize is the smaller of the position's collateral and its debt times the liquidation incentive. The queue is the sum across positions.

  4. Measure what can be sold

    Quote sales of the collateral into the loan asset through live routes on the same chain, at increasing sizes. The largest sale whose proceeds still repay the debt (proceeds of at least the sale divided by the liquidation incentive) is what can be sold. Routes include any on-chain redemption the router reaches.

  5. Grade it

    Coverage is what can be sold divided by the queue, capped at 100%. The grade follows from coverage. Queue and sale are both valued at today's price.

Liquidation incentive (LIF) = min(1.15, 1 / (1 − 0.3 × (1 − LLTV))). At 91.5% LLTV the liquidator gets 2.6% more collateral than the debt it repays, so any sale that loses more than about 2.55% loses money.

Why we divide by the queue, not market cap

Liquidity scores usually compare exit depth with market cap. A lender needs something else: exit capacity against the collateral its own borrowers could force into liquidation.

Committed capacity

Liquidators don't have to sell on a DEX. They can sell to a market maker, redeem with the issuer, or hand the collateral to a buyer who holds it. That capacity is real, but it only counts when it is committed and can be checked. It is shown beside the grade as recognized coverage and never changes the grade itself.

EvidenceWhat it isCounts
Public on-chainExit any router reaches at quote time, including atomic redemptionSets the grade
On-chain committedCapital visible on-chain in a dedicated facility under published rules, such as a funded vault or atomic redemption with cash presentIn full
ContractualA named counterparty with a signed commitment covering size, bid formula, settlement time and tenor, with funding arranged and a completed drillWith a published haircut
Verified redemptionIssuer redemption with documented eligibility, capacity and settlement time, reachable by the party that seizes the collateralUp to documented capacity in the settlement window
DeclaredCounterparties named by the issuer but not committedShown, counted as $0

Two tests apply before anything counts. Capacity must not depend on the same pools or the same cash buffer the shock drains. And capacity that stops when an issuer fails an attestation or suspends redemption covers a liquidity shock, not a credit loss. More liquidity cannot fix a credit loss. No market rated here has registered committed capacity yet. Issuers and lenders can register it through amplifiliquidity.com.

Rules we hold ourselves to

  • One published formula. No analyst overrides, no committee, no manual grades.
  • Nothing estimated goes into a grade. What can't be observed on-chain or in a live quote stays out.
  • Amplifi also builds exit liquidity for issuers. Any market where we operate liquidity is labelled, graded by the same formula, and shown with and without our capacity. Nobody can pay to change a grade.
  • An A or NQ means there is no exit problem to fix, and we say so.
  • When we find an error, we say what it was, withdraw what it affected and publish the correction.

Known limits

  • Positions and prices come from Morpho's API and routes from live quotes, so a rerun moves the numbers. Every raw quote is archived with its time and request.
  • The sale test is cumulative: a little liquidity priced below the liquidator's break-even can count if the average sale still pays. A marginal test is the next version.
  • Pendle PTs are quoted through Pendle's router only.
  • Firms that repay from their own balance sheet and hold the collateral, as one did for most of PT-reUSD's 25 August liquidations, are not counted until committed.
  • Some oracles can't follow the market, such as an issuer's own reported value or a fixed $1 with a depeg delay. For those markets the queue only appears after an issuer event or a sustained depeg. The grade still answers whether the exit holds when that happens.
  • Markets on Katana, Stable are not graded: no aggregator quotes those chains yet. Aave and other lenders are not covered in this edition.
